01 / Use case

Route the incident.
Choose the next check.

A network operations team needs an initial triage decision from the evidence available now. The question: can a small, non-generative model do this as well as an LLM, with less inference overhead?

Evidence

What is known?

Ticket notes, current observations, impact, topology and change records.

Triage

Who investigates next?

Select an owner and diagnostic action. Retain uncertain incidents with NOC.

Handoff

Act on a bounded decision

Set urgency and state whether more evidence is needed. No configuration changes.

Actual synthetic benchmark example

“A blown DC distribution fuse isolates the radio and site router. Independent upstream probes show the aggregation node is healthy.”

Public input

Decision time, observation text and timestamps, service status and site count, a partial topology graph, and change status or scope. The original records also include a ticket.

Models receive facts, policy and allowed decisions. Reference labels, family IDs and rationales stay outside prediction requests.

Paired evidence intervention

Keep fault vocabulary present in both cases. Change one fact: confirmed versus ruled-out fault, stale versus current evidence, connected versus unrelated dependency, or verified versus uncertain recovery.

A complete pair requires both variants to pass.

1,064 records do not mean 1,064 independent scenarios. Each regular family has 20 correlated realizations. All data is AI-assisted synthetic material, and the labels lack independent network-specialist certification.

03 / Policy and baseline

Code handles urgency. Evidence determines the route.

The fictional policy makes priority deterministic when structured impact is reliable. The baseline adds simple keyword routing tailored to these templates.

This calculator demonstrates policy only. It does not run a model or extract impact from ticket text. Site count is ignored for unknown or no current unplanned impact.

PriorityTrigger
P1Outage at 10 or more sites
P2Outage at 1–9 sites, or degradation at 10 or more
P3Other degradation or unknown impact
P4No current unplanned impact

Intended routing policy

Use current, independent evidence for a unique investigating domain. Missing, stale or conflicting evidence stays with NOC. Verified recovery or fully explained maintenance can lead to monitoring.

Change timing and physical proximity alone do not establish cause.

Implemented baseline

  1. Compute priority from structured impact.
  2. No current impact: NOC / monitor.
  3. Unknown impact or uncertainty phrases: NOC / gather evidence or verify change.
  4. Scan observation keywords in order: power, core, transport, radio.
  5. No match: NOC / gather evidence.

Baseline strength depends on the data. Keyword rules score 200/220 on each main split. Each set’s 20 misses come from one family. The implementation does not reason over graph dependencies.

What zero-shot means here

No Northstar training examples or task-specific few-shot examples. Released checkpoints can already have decision training on other tasks.

How a case passes

All four accepted decisions must match. A failed, malformed or missing response counts as wrong. Field scores and complete pairs expose hidden weaknesses.

Typed System One requests and Luna’s chat prompt differ. RLCD is TypeSafe’s training method, not an architecture shared by all candidates. The comparison does not isolate architecture alone.

05 / Zero-shot results

Laya and CLM did not transfer under this protocol.

Rules and Luna led complete decisions. Jev and Kev showed useful capability, but changed rank across the two regular sets.

Test · all four decisions

The weakness is visible by field

Laya and CLM answered “insufficient evidence” on every case. Laya missed all 31 P1 cases; CLM missed 30. These were completed predictions, not failed requests.

The leaders still have limits

Luna’s test lead over rules is just 2/220. Family-grouped uncertainty includes no advantage. Jev and Kev each pass 6/12 challenge pairs, failing the change-timing and topology interventions.

Supported finding: the tested Laya and CLM configurations are ineffective zero-shot here. This does not prove that every checkpoint or a task-trained version will fail.

06 / First encoder and Jev controls

Perfect template scores did not survive harder evidence.

A frozen 22.7M-parameter MiniLM encoder with a trained linear head removes generation at inference. The first result looked strong until we tested new paired mechanisms.

Test · all four decisions

The encoder learns seven owner/check/evidence dispositions from 600 template realizations. Code supplies priority. Its post-hoc policy guards were added after inspecting six challenge errors.

New 16-case development cohort

The original encoder falls to 8/16, including after the later label corrections. Its renderer omits observation timestamps and graph edges. Old test and challenge data were already inspected.

Jev interface changes did not fix transfer

On the original 220-case validation cohort, original Jev with policy priority scores 159/220, compact input 141/220, and decomposition 120/220.

The original and compact policy-priority results are offline diagnostics. A repeated original Jev run includes one counted HTTP failure. These are development comparisons, not the zero-shot table’s original runs.

The next experiment focused on training and representation. Keep failures visible, retain the original configurations, and teach positive, negative, stale and conflicting evidence with separate paired examples.

07 / Teacher training

Use LLMs offline. Serve an encoder locally.

Luna labels public evidence packets. Sol audits selected training families and all new development cases. The deployed classifier needs no autoregressive call.

Offline review

Luna + Sol

Blind to author references and prior predictions. Check evidence citations, schema and policy. Preserve disagreements and every attempt.

Training

MiniLM + head

Frozen 384-dimensional embeddings plus 21 structured flags. Compare text-only and end-to-end fine-tuning.

Inference

Seven-way decision + priority

Read public inputs and local artifacts. Select a supported disposition; code computes urgency from impact.

  1. Improve the renderer and learn from reviewed pairs

    Retain observation age, conflicts, graph edges and change facts. The first accepted teacher training release has 58 incidents; development agreement rises from 8/16 to 13/16.

  2. Check optimization, then reduce regularization

    All five fits converge. L2 = 0.001 reaches 58/58 training and 14/16 development. Lower values do not improve development.

  3. Expand training and test transfer

    Add 60 training and 40 development incidents. Sol audits 16 fixed new training cases and all 40 new development cases. Accepted totals: 118 training and 54 development.

One development pair is excluded. Luna selects core / verify change; Sol selects core / inspect core. The former falls outside the seven coupled dispositions. Both variants remain in the evidence, and 54/54 Luna agreement covers only the accepted cohort.

The latest teachers resolve to Luna 2026-09-22 and Sol 2026-09-29. Initial overlapping reviews hit 24 rate limits; paced continuation completes the reviews. First-valid selection does not consult author labels. No development case enters gradient training, but development selects heads and epochs.

08 / Expanded transfer results

The expanded encoder improves. Luna still leads.

Compare every candidate on the same accepted inputs. These scores measure agreement with teacher-derived labels on inspected development.

Combined development · all four decisions

Aggregate gain hides a routing cost

The expanded head gains six correct cases and loses four versus the previous head: net +2. False domain assignments rise from 1 to 5. False monitoring remains zero.

Fine-tuning is not an automatic win

Seed 17 reaches 36/38 on new cases but 11/16 on old cases. Combined scores are 47/54 and 46/54 for the two seeds. Epochs are selected on combined development.

Structured evidence helps this configuration. The expanded head with 21 flags gets 48/54, versus 41/54 for text-only. Even text-only uses code for priority. The expanded head remains six decisions short of Luna at full coverage.

The expanded-minus-previous family-bootstrap 95% interval is −5.6 to +13.0 percentage points. The sample and development selection prevent a reliable claim of improvement or production accuracy.

12 / Findings and next steps
Keep the encoder path. Do not claim LLM parity yet.
The study establishes a feasible low-latency inference path and a measurable transfer problem. It has not identified a model ready for unattended operational triage.

  
What the evidence supports
  • Unmodified Laya and CLM fail this zero-shot task under the tested protocol.
  • Jev and Kev show useful routing, with significant family sensitivity.
  • A trained small encoder handles repeated templates well and improves with reviewed data and structured evidence.
  • The best expanded encoder reaches 48/54; the separately run Luna classifier reaches 54/54 accepted labels.
What remains unproven
  • Accuracy on real incident notes, incomplete telemetry and operational class frequencies.
  • Independent correctness of teacher-derived labels.
  • Calibrated automation coverage and severe-error bounds.
  • Production cost, cold start, load capacity and handoff quality.

  
Next research steps
  1. Define valid owner/action combinations. The seven-class head cannot express every plausible triage decision.
  2. Freeze the configuration before a new family-separated stress evaluation. Keep old cases for regressions.
  3. Compare richer encoder training and stronger representations on the same new evidence, including negation, contradiction and graph dependencies.
  4. Calibrate acceptance on development, then evaluate coverage and errors on untouched cases.
  5. Obtain de-identified operational evidence and reviewed initial routing/action labels before a service pilot.

  
Laya and CLM follow-up: verify the checkpoint and question interface, check CLM serving parity, then test supported task-specific training. This study trained MiniLM, not Laya or CLM. Keep Jev and Kev as decision-model research candidates; neither consistently matched rules or Luna.

  
Current blocker: representative labeled operational incidents are unavailable. More synthetic tuning can test transfer, but cannot establish production equivalence to Luna or justify deployment for TELUS incidents.
